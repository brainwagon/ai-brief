# Handoff — review the sub-1B retune after a few days

Written 2026-09-06. Pick this up around **2026-09-10**, once four or five
Editions have been Generated by the changed Rubric.

## Where things stand

The Rubric now prefers language models under 1B parameters — both the
announcement of one and a first-hand account of running one. `pick-prompt.md`
also puts such an Item first among the Picks.

All of it is committed and pushed (`8f464da` and the two commits before it). The
timer is armed. Editions dated **2026-09-07 and later** are the first ones the
changed Rubric scored; 2026-09-06 and earlier are the "before" side of every
comparison below.

Five versions of the clause were measured against the 188 Items of
2026-08-17 plus 12 injected controls. `README.md` here has the full account. The
live Rubric measured best of the five: the widest sub-1B separation
(+1.8/+1.0/+1.0 against near-identical larger twins, from +0.6/+0.8/+0.2), and
the boundary traps held where looser drafts leaked.

## Run this first

    .venv/bin/python calibration/2026-09-06-retune/check-editions.py 2026-08-31

It reads `docs/*.html` — what was really published, not a candidate pool — and
prints one line per Edition: Item count, Pick count, Score distribution, and two
counts explained below. Everything in this handoff is read off that output.

## The three questions to answer

### 1. Has the Score 4 gone?

**This is the one that matters most.** In the seven Editions before the change,
Score 4 was 61% of all Items and Score 5 was 0%. The Brief is a page of 4s.

In the pool test, the changed Rubric cut 4s from 12 to 6 out of 188. If that
carries into real Editions, roughly half the page moves from 4 to 3, and a Brief
where nothing scores above 3 has lost the only signal that sorts the page.

Most of that drop comes from the rewritten 3 and 5 paragraphs, not from the
sub-1B rule — the same commit tightened both. Read the `Scores` column across
2026-09-07 onward. A page still mostly 4s is fine. A page that has gone mostly 3s
needs the 3 and 5 paragraphs looked at again, and the sub-1B clause is probably
not the culprit.

### 2. Are sub-1B Items actually arriving, and where do they land?

The `sub-1B` column counts Items whose title or Synopsis states a size under 1B.
The bottom section lists them with their Scores.

Before the change these appeared about one per Edition and already scored 3 or 4
— a 294k-parameter TTS stack, a 90M model on a Sony PSP. So the Source material
exists and the old Rubric did not ignore it.

What to look for: such Items scoring **4 rather than 3**, and appearing among the
Picks. If they still score 3, the clause is not doing its work in the field
whatever the controls said. Note that a Synopsis should now carry the parameter
count and the hardware — check that it does.

### 3. Is the derivative defect real in practice?

The `derivative` column counts Items whose **title** looks like a re-upload
repository — `owner/name-GGUF`, `-FP8`, `-AWQ`, and so on. The Rubric says these
score 2 and never reach a page.

In the controls this leaked badly: a GGUF re-upload of a 360M model gained +0.8
over baseline and crossed the cutoff, though the clause names derivatives
explicitly. Three different phrasings failed to close it, so more words are not
the fix and I did not find the cause.

But in seven real Editions **only one** such Item reached a page. So the defect
may be rare enough to leave alone. Count them after the change. If they are still
about one a week, leave it. If they start filling slots on Hugging Face models,
that is the moment to dig into why the size rule overrides the derivative rule.

Be careful with this column: it deliberately does **not** flag a first-hand
report of somebody *running* a quantised model. Those are the exception the
derivative rule already names and the Rubric wants them. My first version of the
check flagged 21 Items and 20 of them were exactly that.

## Two things never measured

- **`pick-prompt.md`.** The Pick pass was never run in any of this testing. The
  line putting a sub-1B Item first among the Picks is unverified. Question 2
  above is the only evidence that will exist.
- **Four of the six sub-1B controls are worthless.** `handson`, `paper`, `repo`
  and `ceiling` all score 4.8-5.0 under the *baseline* Rubric — hands-on,
  reproducible, small-and-local work the Rubric already loved, with no headroom
  for a point to show in. Only `release/sub1b` and the paired gaps measure
  anything. If the controls get used again, replace those four with sub-1B Items
  that are ordinary on every other axis.

## If the answer is "put it back"

`calibration/2026-09-06-retune/rubric-old.md` is the Rubric as it stood at
2a8113e, before any of this. The sub-1B change is two passages: a sentence on the
**Small and local** bullet, and a clause on **Scale is not merit**. Both can come
out without touching anything else the parallel rewrite did.

Note that reverting `rubric.md` wholesale would also undo that parallel rewrite —
the tightened 3 and 5 paragraphs, popularity demoted to no evidence at all, and
the paper rule. Those were somebody else's decisions, made the same day, and are
not mine to guess at.
